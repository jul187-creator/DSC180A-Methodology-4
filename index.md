**1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

One of the most interesting topics I learned this quarter is how different spring configurations can affect kinetic energy transmission during an impact. Before this project, I did not know much about how nonlinear materials work. I found it interesting that changing the spring coefficients can significantly affect a material's performance, even when the overall structure remains the same.

**2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

For Quarter 2, I would like to investigate how the number of spring families affects the performance of nonlinear spring chains. Our current model uses four spring families, but I think it would be interesting to compare designs with different numbers of spring families. I would like to see whether increasing the number of spring families reduces the KE ratio and improves robustness.

**3. What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

One potential improvement would be to expand the dataset and use a more targeted sampling strategy. Instead of relying only on randomly generated spring parameters, we could focus more on regions of the design space associated with low kinetic energy ratios. This could improve the accuracy of our predictions and make inverse design more efficient.

**4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I would be interested in exploring Bayesian optimization and neural networks. Bayesian optimization could help identify promising spring configurations using fewer expensive simulations, while neural networks might better capture nonlinear relationships between spring coefficients and kinetic energy transmission. I would also like to use visualization techniques to compare model predictions and actual simulation results.
